An 8-day Australia trip from India for two cities, Sydney and Melbourne, costs about ₹1.67 lakh to ₹2.59 lakh per person including flights, and you need a Visitor visa (subclass 600) before you fly. This plan adds the Blue Mountains and the Great Ocean Road as day trips and uses one short internal flight.

## Visa and entry rules for Indians

**Indian passport holders need a visa for Australia; for tourism it is the Visitor visa (subclass 600), applied for through the Department of Home Affairs.** We checked the department's [Visitor visa page](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/visitor-600) on 10 October 2026.

- **What it is.** The page describes the visa as "for tourists, business visitors or to visit family for 3, 6 or 12 months".
- **What you must show.** The two eligibility points it lists are that you are a genuine visitor and that you have enough funds to support your stay and leave. In practice, that means recent bank statements, proof of employment or study and a return ticket.
- **What we could not verify.** The fee and the processing time are loaded dynamically on the Home Affairs site, and we could not read them when we checked. We will not quote a figure we have not seen. Check the department's current visa pricing page and its processing-times tool before you book flights.

The page names three durations, 3, 6 or 12 months, which is the length of the visa you are granted. How long you may stay on each visit is set by the grant, so read the conditions on your visa grant notice.

> Apply only through the official Home Affairs website (homeaffairs.gov.au). Look-alike sites charge extra for forms you can file yourself, and no agent can promise approval.

Flights are the biggest line, and they swing with the season: the late-December holidays cost the most. Sydney hotels are expensive; a room near Central Station or in Chinatown, a short tram or train from the sights, costs less than one at Circular Quay. The Australian dollar is roughly ₹55 to ₹60, but check the day's rate before you convert any budget.

## Day-by-day plan

### Day 1: arrive in Sydney

Most flights from India land early in the morning. Take the airport train to Central, drop your bags and walk down to Circular Quay for your first look at the Opera House and Harbour Bridge. Have an early night; the flight from India is long.

### Day 2: Opera House, The Rocks and the harbour

Take a guided Opera House tour in the morning, then walk through The Rocks and the Royal Botanic Garden to Mrs Macquarie's Chair for the classic view. In the evening, take a ferry to Manly or a harbour cruise at sunset.

### Day 3: Blue Mountains

Take the train from Central to Katoomba, about two hours. Walk to Echo Point for the Three Sisters, then take the Scenic World rides or one of the Giant Stairway walks if the weather is clear. Leave in time to be back in Sydney for dinner.

### Day 4: beaches and the zoo

Walk the coastal path from Bondi to Coogee, then spend the afternoon at Taronga Zoo, a ferry ride from Circular Quay, for a view of the skyline with the animals in front. Dinner in Harris Park, west of the city, is a good option if you want a proper North Indian meal.

### Day 5: fly to Melbourne

Take a morning flight from Sydney to Melbourne, a bit over an hour. Reach the city by SkyBus or taxi, and walk the laneways in the afternoon: Hosier Lane for street art, Degraves Street for coffee, and the State Library. Take the free City Circle tram for a quick orientation.

### Day 6: Melbourne at an easy pace

Visit Queen Victoria Market in the morning and the National Gallery of Victoria or the Royal Botanic Gardens. In the evening, try a tram ride to St Kilda for the beach and the sunset, or watch the penguins at Phillip Island if you can spare the extra day.

### Day 7: Great Ocean Road

Book a full-day group tour from Melbourne, which covers the coast road, a rainforest stop and the Twelve Apostles. It is a long day, roughly 12 hours, so take motion-sickness tablets if you need them. A self-drive takes the same time and needs an international driving permit.

### Day 8: fly home

Take the SkyBus to the airport, three hours before an international flight. Eat a last breakfast in a laneway café before you go.

## Food for vegetarians and Jain travellers

Both cities have a large Indian-origin population, so finding a vegetarian thali is easy. Sydney's Harris Park and Melbourne's suburbs have many Indian restaurants. In mainstream restaurants, vegetarian dishes are marked clearly, but check for eggs and for dishes cooked in shared pans. Jain travellers should call ahead and keep dry snacks for long tour days.

## Best time to go

**September to November** and **March to May** are the best months. **December to February** is summer, hot and busy, with school holidays and the highest prices. **June to August** is winter: cool, with rain in Melbourne, though flights and hotels cost less. Australia is on daylight saving from October to early April.

## Practical tips

- **Money:** use a forex card or a no-markup Visa or Mastercard. Cards and contactless payment are accepted almost everywhere, so you need little cash. We would not rely on UPI.
- **SIM:** an eSIM bought before you fly works on landing.
- **Plugs:** Australia uses the Type I plug at 230 V, so bring an adapter.
- **Transport:** top up an Opal card in Sydney and a myki card in Melbourne, or use contactless cards where accepted.
- **Time:** Sydney and Melbourne are 4 hours 30 minutes ahead of India in the southern winter and 5 hours 30 minutes ahead during daylight saving.
- **Food and customs:** Australia is strict about food, plant and animal products at the border. Declare packed snacks and spices on the arrival card rather than risk a fine.
