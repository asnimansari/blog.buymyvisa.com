Bali suits a first trip abroad: one flight, a visa you collect at the airport, cheap private drivers and a mix of temples, rice fields and beaches. A good first plan is six days split across Ubud, a Nusa Penida day trip and Uluwatu, for roughly ₹55,000 to ₹85,000 per person with flights.

## Visa and entry rules for Indians

**Indian passport holders can get a visa on arrival (VOA) in Bali.** We checked on 9 October 2026. The Yogyakarta immigration office, part of Indonesia's immigration service, lists India among the 97 nationalities eligible for the VOA and states the terms ([how to get a visa on arrival](https://jogja.imigrasi.go.id/how-to-get-an-indonesia-visa-on-arrival/)):

- **Fee:** IDR 500,000, payable in US dollars or rupiah. At roughly ₹5.3 per 1,000 rupiah that is about ₹2,650.
- **Stay:** up to 30 days, extendable once for another 30 days at an immigration office (the extension is also IDR 500,000).
- **Where:** designated airports, seaports and land posts, including Bali's Ngurah Rai airport.
- **Online option:** you can apply for an e-VOA in advance on the official [eVisa portal](https://evisa.imigrasi.go.id/), which lets you skip the VOA counter queue.

The same page doesn't state passport validity or the onward-ticket rule. Secondary sources report that Indonesia expects a passport valid for at least six months and a return or onward ticket; plan for both, and confirm with the portal.

**Fee warning:** in August 2026 Indonesia's immigration director general proposed raising the e-VOA to IDR 750,000 and the on-arrival VOA to IDR 1,000,000. We found no effective date in the reports we could read, so check the portal for the current figure before you pay.

**Arrival card:** the free **All Indonesia** arrival card at [allindonesia.imigrasi.go.id](https://allindonesia.imigrasi.go.id/) covers immigration, customs and health declarations, and is reportedly filed in the 72 hours before landing. We couldn't open the portal from our server, so read its instructions yourself.

**Tourist levy:** Bali also charges foreign visitors a one-time levy, reported at IDR 150,000 per person, through the province's Love Bali portal. We could not verify the amount officially.

> Use only the official government sites above. Look-alike "Indonesia visa" and "arrival card" websites add their own fees; if a site charges you to file the free arrival card, close it.

## 6-day Bali itinerary

### Day 1: arrive and drive to Ubud

Clear immigration at Ngurah Rai, collect your bags and pre-book a driver or use a ride app from the official pick-up zone. Ubud is about 1.5 hours away, depending on traffic. Spend the evening walking the centre, and have dinner at a warung.

### Day 2: rice terraces, temples and a swing

Start early at the **Tegallalang rice terraces** before the crowds. Add a temple such as **Tirta Empul**, where visitors join a purification ritual in sarong and sash (rented at the gate), and stop for coffee near the fields. In the afternoon, visit the **Sacred Monkey Forest** and keep bags zipped and sunglasses off, because the macaques grab things.

### Day 3: slow day in Ubud

Take a cooking class, a short Campuhan Ridge Walk at sunrise, or a waterfall such as Tegenungan. Evenings are good for a Balinese dance performance at the Ubud Palace. This is your buffer day for a late start or rain.

### Day 4: Nusa Penida day trip

Most tours collect you from Ubud before dawn, take the fast boat from Sanur, and return in the evening. Highlights are **Kelingking Beach**, the T-Rex shaped cliff, and **Angel's Billabong**. Roads on the island are steep and rough, so don't hire a scooter unless you are experienced. If seas are rough, boats are cancelled, so keep this day flexible.

### Day 5: move to Uluwatu

Transfer to the Bukit peninsula in the morning (about 1.5 to 2 hours). Spend the day on a beach such as **Padang Padang** or **Bingin**, then watch sunset at **Uluwatu Temple** and the Kecak fire dance. Keep your phone and glasses secure on the temple grounds, because the monkeys here steal too.

### Day 6: last swim, then fly home

Have a slow breakfast, and book a late-evening flight if you can. Reach the airport at least three hours before departure; allow extra time for traffic from Uluwatu, which is typically 45 to 60 minutes from the airport.

## Tips for first-timers

- **Dress and temples:** carry a scarf or sarong, cover shoulders and knees, and remove shoes where asked. Don't step on the small offerings (canang sari) left on pavements.
- **Drivers:** a private driver for a day is a standard way to see Bali; agree the price and hours first. Ride apps are limited in some areas.
- **Money:** carry a forex card and some rupiah in cash. UPI isn't generally accepted, and standalone ATMs are best avoided.
- **SIM:** an eSIM bought before you fly is easier than a counter SIM.
- **Water and food:** drink bottled water and avoid ice in small stalls if your stomach is sensitive.
- **Plugs:** Indonesia uses the two-round-pin Type C/F plug at 230 V, so carry an adapter.
- **Insurance:** buy travel insurance that covers scooter use if you plan to ride; many policies don't.

## Best time to go

**May to September** is the dry season, with sun and cooler nights, and overlaps Indian summer holidays, so book early. **October to April** brings the wet season, with afternoon downpours and lower prices; Nusa Penida boats are most often cancelled in rougher weather.
