A 6-day Bhutan trip from India costs roughly ₹50,000 to ₹75,000 per person for two people sharing, with flights. Indians need no visa, only an entry permit and a daily fee of ₹1,200 per adult, which makes Bhutan far cheaper for you than for most foreigners. This plan covers Paro, Thimphu and Punakha at an easy pace, with a rupee breakdown.

## Visa and entry rules for Indians

**There is no visa for Indian citizens, but you need an entry permit and must pay the Sustainable Development Fee (SDF).** These details come from Bhutan's official tourism FAQ at [bhutan.travel/faqs](https://www.bhutan.travel/faqs), checked on 8 October 2026.

- **ID:** an Indian passport or an Indian voter ID card. Indians under 18 can travel on a passport or birth certificate, accompanied by a legal guardian.
- **Permit:** apply online before you travel, through the visa page linked from the tourism site (the immigration portal is [immi.gov.bt](https://www.immi.gov.bt/)). You can also apply at the point of entry, but the official site warns of a possible long wait, so do it in advance. The FAQ states no permit fee for Indians, so ask whoever books your trip what, if anything, is added.
- **SDF:** Nu 1,200 (or the same in rupees) per adult per night. Children aged 6 to under 12 pay 600 a night, and children under 6 pay nothing.
- **Length of stay:** the FAQ gives 90 days for a visa and doesn't give a separate limit for Indians, so confirm your permit dates.
- **Phuentsholing:** a border-town waiver, running from 2 June 2026 until August 2027, means no SDF if you stay in Phuentsholing up to 24 hours. It ends at the Rinchending checkpoint. Going further means paying SDF.

> Use only the official sites above. Look-alike permit and "visa" sites charge extra for a free form, so check the web address before entering your details or paying.

Flights are the largest and most variable line. Drukair and Bhutan Airlines fly into Paro, the country's only international airport, and fares rise sharply in March to May and October to November. Entering by road through Phuentsholing, reached from Bagdogra or New Jalpaiguri, lowers the fare but adds a long drive each way (about six hours from Phuentsholing to Thimphu), which eats into a 6-day plan. Tour operators advertise 6-day packages from roughly ₹40,000, but these usually leave out flights, so compare like with like.

## 6-day itinerary

### Day 1: arrive in Paro

The flight into Paro is itself a sight, with the plane threading between mountains. Complete immigration, check in and keep the afternoon easy: the **Rinpung Dzong**, the nearby covered bridge and Paro town. Take it slowly on day one because Paro sits above 2,200 m.

### Day 2: Tiger's Nest

Start early for **Paro Taktsang**, the monastery on the cliff. Allow about four to six hours return on a marked trail, with a tea stop partway. Wear proper shoes, carry water and keep the afternoon free.

### Day 3: Paro to Thimphu

Drive to the capital in roughly an hour and a half. See the **Memorial Chorten**, the **Buddha Dordenma** statue, a handicraft market and the weekend market if you arrive on a Saturday or Sunday. Dinner on Norzin Lam, the main street, is easy.

### Day 4: Thimphu to Punakha

Cross the **Dochula Pass** (about 3,100 m) for Himalayan views on clear days, then continue to **Punakha**, where the valley is warm and green. The **Punakha Dzong**, at the meeting of two rivers, is the highlight, and the suspension bridge nearby is among the longest in Bhutan.

### Day 5: Punakha

Visit **Chimi Lhakhang**, the fertility temple, via a walk through paddy fields, or add a gentle rafting trip on the river if the season allows. If you want a night in the Phobjikha valley, you need another day.

### Day 6: back to Paro and fly home

Drive back to Paro, around four to five hours, and keep a buffer for your flight, as schedules can change with the weather.

## Tips for Indian travellers

- **Money:** rupees are accepted widely, and the official site tells Indian travellers to bring cards enabled for India, Bhutan and Nepal, as USD cards don't work for them. Withdraw cash in towns, as small shops may not take UPI.
- **Phone:** check roaming charges, or buy a Bhutanese SIM in Paro or Thimphu.
- **Food:** vegetarian dishes such as ema datshi (chillies and cheese) are everywhere, and Indian food is easy to find in towns.
- **Altitude:** drink water and don't overexert on day one.
- **Dress:** layers, even in spring, and modest clothes for dzongs. Photos are often banned inside temples.
- **Plugs:** Bhutan uses 230 V sockets that commonly take Indian-style round pins, but carry a universal adapter to be safe.

## Best time to go

**March to May** and **September to November** are the clearest and most popular months, and many tshechu festivals fall then. **June to August** is the monsoon, with road landslides possible. **December to February** is cold at night but sunny, with fewer visitors.
