Dubai is one of the easiest first international trips with children from India: a flight of about three and a half hours, no jet lag worth mentioning, rupee-friendly vegetarian food and an itinerary that works in the heat of the day. Five days is enough, and a family should budget roughly ₹56,000 to ₹87,000 per person including flights. The step to get right first is the visa, because most Indians cannot get one at the airport.

## Visa and entry rules for Indians

**Most Indian passport holders need a UAE visa before they fly.** The route is a visit visa issued through the GDRFA Dubai portal or ICP smart services, or booked via an airline or an agent. GDRFA Dubai's [single-entry tourist visa page](https://www.gdrfad.gov.ae/en/services/f9e586fe-0642-11ec-0320-0050569629e8) lists:

1. **Fee:** AED 200 for a 30-day visa or AED 300 for 60 days, plus 5% VAT, with no security deposit.
2. **Processing:** an expected completion time of 48 hours. Airlines and agents often quote longer, so apply at least a week ahead, and earlier in school holidays.
3. **Documents:** a passport valid for at least six months, one personal photo and a copy of the passport. The page also lists a ticket to continue your journey or leave the UAE, and medical insurance valid in the UAE, among its conditions.
4. **One visa per traveller**, children and infants included.

**Visa on arrival** exists only for a narrow group. GDRFA's [visa-on-arrival page for Indian citizens](https://gdrfad.gov.ae/en/services/727c91b1-52eb-11ea-0320-0050569629e8) covers holders of an ordinary Indian passport with a US green card, or a UK or EU residence visa, and grants 14 days, extendable once for a similar period. The UAE embassy in New Delhi announced in February 2025 that residence permits from Australia, Canada, Japan, New Zealand, South Korea and Singapore also qualify; we could not open that page again today, so confirm with the embassy before relying on it. If you hold none of these, apply in advance.

> Apply through the [GDRFA Dubai](https://www.gdrfad.gov.ae/), the [ICP](https://icp.gov.ae/en/) or your airline. Look-alike visa sites are common: the official fee is a few hundred dirhams, so be wary of any quote far above what an airline or well-known agent charges, and never send passport copies to an unverified website.

For Indian rupee planning, AED 200 plus VAT is roughly ₹5,000 at ₹24 per dirham. Expect to pay more through an airline or agent. The dirham is pegged to the US dollar, so check the day's rate.

Rooms, airport transfers and a shared data plan don't multiply by headcount, so a family of four pays less per head than a couple would. Attraction prices change often and vary by date and time slot, so check the operators' own sites before you book; a Burj Khalifa ticket for a sunset slot costs noticeably more than a mid-morning one.

## Day-by-day family itinerary

### Day 1: arrive and Dubai Marina

Land, clear immigration and check in near the **Marina** or **JBR**, where the beach, the walkway and children's play areas are within a short walk of hotels. Keep the first day light: a swim, an early dinner at the Marina Walk and bed.

### Day 2: Downtown Dubai

Go up the **Burj Khalifa** early or at sunset, booked online for a fixed slot. In the afternoon, the **Dubai Aquarium** at Dubai Mall is easy to combine with lunch and shopping. Finish by the Dubai Fountain, which plays in the evening and is free to watch from the lakeside promenade.

### Day 3: Old Dubai, the Creek and the Frame

Cross the **Creek** by abra, the wooden water taxi, for a few dirhams, then walk through the Al Fahidi historic district and the spice and gold souks. Ride the metro or a taxi to **Dubai Frame** for a view of both old and new Dubai. In the evening, a **dhow dinner cruise** on the Creek or at the Marina suits children who like boats.

### Day 4: desert safari

Book an afternoon-to-evening desert safari with a licensed operator. A typical package includes dune driving, camel rides, a sunset stop and a barbecue dinner with a show. Ask about age and height rules, request a vegetarian dinner when booking, and tell the driver if anyone is prone to motion sickness. Spend the morning at the hotel pool to keep energy up.

### Day 5: spare day, or Abu Dhabi

If your flight is late, use the day for **Abu Dhabi**, about 90 minutes by road: the **Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque** (modest clothing required, covered shoulders and knees, and a head covering for women) and, for theme-park fans, Yas Island. Otherwise, visit a mall or the beach, and leave for the airport at least three hours before the flight.

## Tips for families from India

- **Food:** vegetarian, Jain and Indian restaurants are everywhere, and hotels cater to Indian breakfast preferences. Ask when booking a safari or cruise.
- **Money:** use a forex or multi-currency card, and carry some dirhams for abras, taxis and small stalls. UPI works at some larger merchants but isn't dependable.
- **Plugs:** the UAE uses the Type G (UK-style) plug, so pack an adapter.
- **Transport:** the Dubai Metro and trams are clean and cheap; buy a Nol card at any station. Taxis and ride-hailing apps are plentiful, and a family of four rarely needs a rental car.
- **SIM:** buy a tourist eSIM or SIM at the airport, or turn on an international roaming pack before you leave.
- **Rules:** public conduct is regulated: dress modestly in malls and mosques, don't photograph strangers without asking, and keep medicines in their original packaging with the prescription. Check the current import rules for any prescription drug.

## Best time to go

**November to March** is the sweet spot: warm, dry and comfortable for the beach and the desert. Rates peak around Christmas and New Year, and the Diwali week is also expensive. October and March are cheaper and still pleasant. From June to September the heat makes outdoor plans hard with small children, so if you must go in summer, choose indoor attractions and shift outings to the evening.

## Getting there from India

Emirates, flydubai, Air India, IndiGo and Air Arabia fly to Dubai from most Indian metros and many smaller cities, usually in three to four hours.
