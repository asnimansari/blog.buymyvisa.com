Georgia is a mountain-and-wine trip that costs less than most of Western Europe: three nights split between Tbilisi and Kazbegi, a wine day in Kakheti, and an e-visa you apply for online. Budget roughly ₹65,000 to ₹1,00,000 per person for six days with flights, and start the visa two to three weeks ahead.

## Visa and entry rules for Indians

**Indian passport holders apply for an e-Visa on the Georgian government's portal.** The Foreign Ministry's [e-Visa portal](https://www.evisa.gov.ge/), which we checked on 9 October 2026, has India in its nationality list. Applications are for short stays (under 90 days). Key points from the portal and its FAQ:

1. **Fee:** US$20 plus a 2% service fee. The application page also lists a US$15 identity-verification step, so budget about US$37 in total, or roughly ₹3,000.
2. **Processing:** the FAQ says a decision takes "not less then 5 working days". Do not buy non-refundable tickets until it arrives.
3. **Stay:** the Law on the Legal Status of Aliens caps a short-term single-entry visa at 30 days. You request the number of days on the application, and the approved visa shows what you got.
4. **Documents:** a country-specific list on the portal for India shows a biometric photo, a passport copy, proof of lodging, a round-trip ticket, travel insurance and proof of funds. The passport must be valid for at least three months beyond the visa period and have two blank pages.
5. **At the border:** the portal says to be ready to show these documents on arrival. Print them or keep them offline.

The portal states that the Ministry has no link to private companies offering to submit applications. Apply only on evisa.gov.ge, and be wary of look-alike sites that add large "service" fees. Fee levels and eligibility can change, so confirm on the portal before you pay. We could not open the Indian embassy's page on the e-visa, so the portal is our only source here.

## Day-by-day itinerary

### Day 1: arrive in Tbilisi

Many flights from India with one stop land in the early morning or at night. Check in, then walk the **old town** around Abanotubani and the Meidan area. Eat at a local restaurant for your first khachapuri.

### Day 2: Tbilisi and Mtskheta

Take the cable car up to **Narikala Fortress** in the morning, then a taxi to **Mtskheta**, the old capital about 30 minutes away, for the Jvari Monastery and Svetitskhoveli Cathedral (modest dress for churches). Come back for an evening in the sulphur-bath district.

### Day 3: the Georgian Military Highway to Kazbegi

Hire a driver for the drive north. Stop at **Ananuri Fortress** on the Zhinvali reservoir and the **Gudauri** viewpoint, then reach **Stepantsminda** (Kazbegi) by afternoon. Allow about three hours of driving each way, more if there is traffic or snow at the pass.

### Day 4: Gergeti Trinity Church

Start early for the **Gergeti Trinity Church**, set high on a ridge below Mount Kazbek. You can hike up in about two hours or take a 4x4 from the village. Keep the afternoon free in case cloud hides the views, then drive back to Tbilisi.

### Day 5: Kakheti wine day

Spend a day in **Kakheti**, about two hours east: the walled town of **Sighnaghi**, a cellar visit with qvevri (clay-jar) winemaking, and lunch at a family winery. If you don't drink, the villages, **Bodbe Monastery** and churchyard views still make the trip worthwhile.

### Day 6: fly home

Allow time for the airport, three hours before an international departure.

## Vegetarian and Jain eating in Georgia

Georgia is easier than expected for vegetarians. Look for **khachapuri** (cheese bread), **lobio** (bean stew), **badrijani nigvziani** (aubergine rolls with walnut paste), **pkhali** (vegetable and walnut balls), mushrooms baked in clay pots and **ajapsandali** (vegetable stew). Many soups and khinkali are meat-filled, so ask whether stock or fat has been used. Strict Jain travellers will find it hard to avoid onion and garlic, so carry dry snacks and use hotels with kitchens for some meals.

## Best time to go

**May, June, September and October** are the safest months: mild in Tbilisi, with mountain roads usually open. July and August are hot in the city but pleasant in the mountains. In winter, Gudauri is a ski town and the road to Kazbegi can close in heavy snow, so keep a spare day.

## Practical tips

- **Money:** Georgia uses the lari. Carry a forex card and some US dollars or euros to exchange, and don't plan on UPI.
- **SIM:** an eSIM bought before you fly saves time at the airport.
- **Getting around:** Bolt works in Tbilisi. For Kazbegi and Kakheti, a hired driver is easier than shared marshrutka vans.
- **Plugs:** Georgia uses the European two-pin plug, so bring an adapter.
- **Insurance:** the portal lists travel insurance among the documents you may be asked to show, so buy it before you apply.
