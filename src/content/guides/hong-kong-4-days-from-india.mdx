You can see Hong Kong's main sights in four days: the Peak at night, a Star Ferry ride, Kowloon's markets and the Big Buddha on Lantau. Indian passport holders enter visa-free for up to 14 days, but only after a free online **Pre-arrival Registration (PAR)**, so do that before you buy a ticket. Budget roughly ₹55,000 to ₹85,000 per person.

## Visa and entry rules for Indians

**No visa for short visits, but you must register first.** The Hong Kong Immigration Department's page on [Pre-arrival Registration for Indian nationals](https://www.immd.gov.hk/eng/services/visas/pre-arrival_registration_for_indian_nationals.html), which we checked on 9 October 2026 (the page shows it was last revised on 27 November 2025), says:

1. **Eligibility:** an Indian passport valid for at least six months, and a visit or transit of up to 14 days.
2. **Fee:** free, through the online service.
3. **Processing:** you register on the designated GovHK website and the result is shown instantly.
4. **Validity:** six months. In that period you can make several visits of up to 14 days each with the same notification slip and the same passport. If you renew or replace your passport, you must register again.
5. **At boarding and arrival:** print the notification slip on blank A4 paper, sign it, and carry it with the passport linked to the registration. Missing either one can mean refused boarding or refused entry.
6. **Exact match:** details must match the passport. A mismatch makes the registration invalid.
7. **No guarantee of entry:** immigration can still refuse entry if you can't show adequate funds, an onward or return ticket, or a genuine purpose of visit. Carry your hotel booking, return ticket and a bank statement or forex card balance.

Stays over 14 days, or any work or study, need a proper visa from the Immigration Department. The page also lists exemptions such as holders of a valid Hong Kong entry visa or the HKSAR Travel Pass. Use only the government route and be careful with look-alike sites that charge a "service fee" for a registration that costs nothing. We did not check any rules for Macau or mainland China, which are separate; confirm them on the official sites if you plan to cross.

Hotels are the main swing: rooms near Tsim Sha Tsui or Mong Kok are small, and prices jump during trade fairs and holidays. Sharing a room halves that line. Transport and ticket prices change, so check them on the operators' sites before you go.

## Day-by-day itinerary

### Day 1: arrive and the Peak at night

Take the Airport Express to Hong Kong Station in Central; a single costs roughly HK$130, and group tickets for several people are cheaper per head. After you check in, ride the **Peak Tram** up to Victoria Peak and walk the Peak Circle Walk, a flat loop of about an hour. Be at the top before sunset and stay for the lights. It is the view in the lead photo.

### Day 2: Kowloon and the harbour

Ride the **Star Ferry** across Victoria Harbour, an old crossing that costs very little. Walk the Tsim Sha Tsui waterfront and the Avenue of Stars, then spend the afternoon in **Mong Kok** at the Ladies' Market, Sneaker Street and the Goldfish Market. At 8 pm, the **Symphony of Lights** harbour show runs from the Kowloon side. Check whether it is running on your date.

### Day 3: Lantau and the Big Buddha

Take the MTR to Tung Chung and the **Ngong Ping 360 cable car** to Ngong Ping, then climb the steps to the **Tian Tan Buddha** and walk to Po Lin Monastery. The monastery's vegetarian restaurant is an easy lunch. Cable car operations can pause for maintenance or wind, so check the operator's website on the morning and keep a bus option to Ngong Ping as a back-up.

### Day 4: Hong Kong Island and fly home

Spend the morning in **Central and Sheung Wan**: Man Mo Temple, the Mid-Levels escalator and Hollywood Road. Eat dim sum for lunch, then go to the airport. Allow three hours before an international departure, and check in with the airport rail's in-town check-in if your airline offers it.

## Eating in Hong Kong as an Indian traveller

Vegetarians can eat well but must ask. Buddhist vegetarian restaurants, such as the one at Po Lin, serve meat-free dishes, and many dim sum places have a few vegetable dumplings, though they may be steamed with lard or stock, so ask. Jain food is hard to find, so carry dry snacks. For halal and Indian food, Chungking Mansions in Tsim Sha Tsui has many Indian and Pakistani restaurants and shops, and is a practical fallback after a long flight.

## Best time to go

**October to December** is the best window: dry, mild and clear. **March** is also pleasant. **June to September** is hot and humid, with typhoon warnings that can disrupt ferries and flights. **January and February** can be cold and damp, so pack a jacket. Avoid Chinese New Year if you want normal hotel prices.

## Practical tips

- **Money:** use a forex card for most spends and keep some Hong Kong dollars for small restaurants. UPI is not widely accepted.
- **Octopus card:** buy one at the airport or an MTR station and use it on trains, buses, ferries and trams. Prices and deposits for the tourist version vary, so check at the counter.
- **SIM:** an eSIM bought before you fly saves queueing at the airport.
- **Plugs:** Hong Kong uses the UK three-pin plug (Type G), so pack an adapter.
- **Walking:** the city is hilly and humid. Wear comfortable shoes and carry a light rain jacket.
- **Paperwork:** keep your PAR slip, passport, hotel booking and return ticket together in one folder.
