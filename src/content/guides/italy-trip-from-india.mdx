For a first trip to Italy, fly into Rome, take trains to Florence and then Venice, and fly home from Venice. Budget roughly ₹1.4 to ₹1.95 lakh per person for eight days including flights, and start the Schengen visa at least six weeks before you fly.

## Visa and entry rules for Indians

**Indian passport holders need a Schengen short-stay visa (type C) for tourism in Italy, applied for in advance.** The Italian Embassy in New Delhi's [visa page](https://ambnewdelhi.esteri.it/en/servizi-consolari-e-visti/servizi-per-il-cittadino-straniero/visti/), which we checked on 10 October 2026, says:

1. **Where to apply:** through VFS Visa Application Centres, by appointment, in New Delhi, Jalandhar or Chandigarh; residents of other states are directed to the consulates in Kolkata, Mumbai or Bangalore. The embassy says using a Visa Application Centre is not mandatory, and the Visa Section makes all decisions.
2. **Fee:** €90 for adults, €45 for ages 6 to under 12, free under 6. The page says rupee amounts vary with the exchange rate. VFS charges a service fee on top, which we could not confirm from the embassy page, so read it off VFS before booking an appointment.
3. **How early:** not earlier than six months before the intended journey.
4. **Processing:** decisions on C visas are generally reached within 15 calendar days, and some cases take longer.
5. **Passport:** residual validity of at least 3 months after the intended stay, and at least 2 blank pages.

The embassy page does not list the supporting documents for a tourist visa. It refers applicants to the Italian foreign ministry's "Visa for Italy" website and to the checklist at VFS, so follow that checklist exactly. In practice it covers flight and hotel reservations, travel insurance with the minimum cover the Schengen rules require, bank statements and an income-tax return; confirm each item on the current checklist.

> Apply only through VFS or the embassy. Look-alike "Italy visa" sites charge large fees for the same appointment. Book your flight only on a refundable or flexible fare, or use a dummy itinerary where the checklist allows it, until the visa is in hand.

Once you hold a Schengen visa you can also add a day trip or a few days in a neighbouring Schengen country; for the same process on another European trip, see our [Switzerland honeymoon guide](/blog/guides/switzerland-honeymoon-from-india/) and [Iceland guide](/blog/guides/iceland/).

Hotels charge a city tax per person per night, usually paid at check-out and not included in the booking price. The amount depends on the city and the hotel's star rating, so check it on your booking.

## Day-by-day 8-day Italy itinerary

### Day 1: arrive in Rome

Aim for a hotel near **Termini** station or in the **Monti** district, so the metro, airport train and the Florence train are all close. Take a slow walk to the **Trevi Fountain** and the **Pantheon** in the evening, when the crowds thin.

### Day 2: ancient Rome

Book a timed entry for the **Colosseum**, **Roman Forum** and **Palatine Hill**, which are usually sold together, and go in the first slot. Eat lunch in **Monti**, then walk to the **Capitoline Hill** for the view over the Forum at sunset.

### Day 3: Vatican and Trastevere

Pick the first **Vatican Museums** slot you can book, then see the Sistine Chapel and St Peter's Basilica. Dress with covered shoulders and knees, or you will be turned away at the basilica. Spend the evening in **Trastevere** for dinner.

### Day 4: train to Florence

Take a high-speed train from Roma Termini to Firenze Santa Maria Novella, which takes about 1 hour 30 minutes. Walk to the **Duomo**, the **Ponte Vecchio** and the **Piazzale Michelangelo** at sunset for the best view of the city.

### Day 5: Florence museums

Book the **Uffizi Gallery** for the morning and the **Accademia** (Michelangelo's David) after lunch. If you would rather skip museums, ride a train or bus into the Tuscan hills for a half-day wine tour instead.

### Day 6: train to Venice

Take the train to Venezia Santa Lucia, about two hours from Florence, and arrive on the Grand Canal by vaporetto (water bus). Stay in the historic centre if you can, because you can then see St Mark's and the Rialto after the day-trippers leave.

### Day 7: Venice and the islands

Visit **St Mark's Square** and the **Doge's Palace** early, then take a vaporetto to **Murano** for glass and **Burano** for its coloured houses. A gondola ride costs a set fee per boat, not per person, so share one with a group.

Venice has introduced an access fee for day-trippers on selected dates. It does not apply to guests who stay overnight in the city, but check the city's official page before you go.

### Day 8: fly home

Take the airport water bus or a bus to **Venice Marco Polo** airport and fly home from there. Flying into Rome and out of Venice, an open-jaw ticket, saves a day of backtracking.

## Tips for Indian travellers

- **Vegetarian and Jain food:** pizza margherita, pasta, risotto and caprese are easy. Ask "senza carne" (without meat) and check for meat stock in soups. Strict Jain travellers should note that onion and garlic are common in sauces.
- **Money:** carry a forex card or a multi-currency card and use contactless payment. Don't rely on UPI at Italian shops and restaurants.
- **Coperto:** many restaurants add a small cover charge per person, which is normal and not a scam.
- **Plugs:** Italy uses Type L and Type C plugs; a Type C adapter, as in most of Europe, works for most sockets.
- **SIM:** an eSIM bought before you fly saves queueing at the airport.
- **Pickpockets:** busy trains, the Rome metro and Venice's vaporettos are hotspots, so carry a cross-body bag.
- **Trains:** book high-speed Frecciarossa or Italo trains ahead for lower fares, and validate regional tickets at the platform machine before boarding.

## Best time to go

**April to May and September to October** are the sweet spot, with mild weather and fewer queues than peak summer. July and August are hot and crowded in Rome and Florence, and winter is cheap but grey. Venice floods occasionally in autumn and winter, so keep a day's slack in the plan.
