An 8-day Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka trip in cherry blossom season costs roughly ₹1.35 to ₹1.9 lakh per person from India, and the part that needs the most planning is the visa: Indians must file for a Japan visa in advance, up to 89 days before travel. Blossom peaks around late March to early April, so for a late-March trip your visa window opens around late December.

## Visa and entry rules for Indians

**You need a visa before you fly.** Japan has no visa on arrival for Indian passport holders. The tourist ("temporary visitor") visa is filed at a Japan Visa Application Centre run by VFS Global. We checked the [VFS Global Japan page for India](https://www.vfsglobal.com/one-pager/japan/india/Chennai/index.html) on 9 October 2026, and it says:

1. **Fee:** INR 500 for single or multiple entry for Indian nationals, effective 1 April 2026. It is refunded only if the consulate does not approve the visa.
2. **Service charge:** INR 800 per application, charged on top and non-refundable. An optional courier return for the passport is INR 550 at some centres (Chennai, Hyderabad, Puducherry and Cochin are listed).
3. **Processing:** a minimum of 3 working days from submission to the consulate, which VFS calls indicative only. It can be longer.
4. **When to apply:** up to 89 days before the intended travel date and not earlier.
5. **Stay:** the tourist visa allows a stay of up to 90 days; the consulate decides the length it grants.
6. **Documents:** the VFS checklist for a tourist visa without a guarantor includes a passport with more than two blank pages (and old passports), a filled and signed application form, a covering letter with purpose and a typed day-by-day schedule, one 2 x 2 inch photo taken in the last 6 months, travel bookings and proof of accommodation, the last 3 years of income tax returns, the latest 3 months of bank statements, and a certificate of employment with leave sanction. It states that reservations are acceptable and recommends not buying confirmed tickets or hotels before the visa is granted.

Applicants who are students, dependants or company owners have extra documents in the same checklist, such as a sponsor letter and the sponsor's returns.

Two things we could not confirm from official sources. Some agency sites say Japan has an eVISA route for Indian applicants; the Embassy of Japan in India's page returned an error when we tried it, and the VFS page we read describes only the in-person route. Check the [Embassy of Japan in India](https://www.in.emb-japan.go.jp/) site before you apply, and trust only it and VFS for forms and fees. We also did not check Japan's arrival procedures such as customs or immigration registration, so look those up before you fly. Beware of look-alike "Japan visa" sites that charge much more than the INR 500 plus INR 800 above; agents may legitimately charge a fee to submit for you, so ask for the total in writing.

## What it costs in rupees

Per person, two sharing a room, mid-range, 8 days and 7 nights in late March to early April. These are our estimates, and yen has moved a lot in recent months. We used roughly ₹0.60 per yen, which is the range quoted by rate sites in September 2026, so check the day's rate.

Flights and hotels are the swing items because March and April are peak months in Japan. Book the flight 3 to 4 months ahead and the hotels as soon as you have a forecast window. Fly into Tokyo and out of Osaka (an open-jaw ticket) so you do not backtrack.

## Cherry blossom season: when and where

Blossom (sakura) usually reaches its peak in **Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka between late March and early April**, and a tree is at full bloom for only about a week. The date moves every year with the winter weather. Forecasts from the Japan Meteorological Agency and private weather companies start appearing around January and change until the week itself, so keep your flights flexible if you can and pick Kyoto or Tokyo days around the latest forecast.

Plan for **falling petals (hanafubuki)** if you arrive a week late: they are just as photogenic.

## Day-by-day itinerary

### Days 1–3: Tokyo

Arrive at Narita or Haneda and take the airport train to your hotel. Spend the first afternoon in **Ueno Park**, where the trees line the central path, and the evening in **Asakusa** near Sensō-ji. On day 2 walk the **Meguro River**, a canal lined with blossom, then go to **Shibuya** and **Shinjuku Gyoen**, a large garden with a ticket and dozens of varieties that bloom over several weeks. Day 3 can be a day in **Harajuku, Meiji Shrine** and the **Tokyo Skytree** or **Shibuya Sky**; book the observation deck a few days ahead.

### Days 4–7: Kyoto and Nara

Take the **Tokaido Shinkansen** from Tokyo to Kyoto, about 2 hours 15 minutes on the fastest train. Reserve a seat, and ask for the right-hand side for a view of Mt Fuji if the weather is clear. In Kyoto:

- **Day 4:** **Philosopher's Path**, the canal walk in the lead photo, and **Ginkaku-ji**; end at the **Gion** district.
- **Day 5:** **Fushimi Inari** at dawn, then **Kiyomizu-dera** and **Maruyama Park**.
- **Day 6:** day trip to **Nara** by train to see the deer in Nara Park and Tōdai-ji.
- **Day 7:** **Arashiyama** (the bamboo grove and the Togetsukyo bridge), then to Osaka in the evening.

### Days 7–8: Osaka and fly home

Osaka is for food: **Dotonbori**, takoyaki and okonomiyaki. Visit **Osaka Castle** for blossom in the morning of day 8 if your flight is in the evening, and take the airport train to Kansai International. Keep two hours free for the trip back to the airport.

## Eating in Japan as an Indian traveller

Vegetarian travellers should know that **dashi**, a fish stock, goes into most soups, sauces and even some vegetable dishes. Ask for "niku nashi, sakana nashi" (no meat, no fish), but expect a restaurant to say it is hard. Kyoto's **shojin ryori** temple meals and the Buddhist vegetarian restaurants near Tenryū-ji are reliable. Indian restaurants are common in Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka, and convenience stores sell onigiri with umeboshi or kombu. Jain travellers should carry dry food for the first days.

## Best time to go

**Late March to early April** is cherry blossom and the busiest time of year: expect crowds and high prices. **November** brings autumn leaves and has similar crowds. **October** is mild and quieter. **June to July** is the rainy season, **July to September** is hot and humid with typhoons, and **December to February** is cold, with snow in the north. If you cannot get the blossom window, a November trip is the closest alternative.

## Practical tips

- **Money:** use a forex card and carry yen cash. Cash is still common at small restaurants, shrines and ticket machines.
- **IC card:** get a Suica or Pasmo on the phone wallet if your phone supports it, or buy one at a station. It works across cities on trains, buses and in many shops.
- **Rail pass:** the nationwide JR Pass raised its prices sharply in October 2023 and rarely pays off on a Tokyo to Kyoto to Osaka route, so price single tickets before you buy.
- **SIM:** an eSIM bought before you fly saves time at the airport.
- **Plugs:** Japan uses a 100 V supply with two flat-pin plugs (Type A). Most Indian phone and laptop chargers work, but check that yours says 100–240 V.
- **Etiquette:** carry your rubbish with you, because public bins are rare; keep your voice low on trains.
- **Luggage:** use a luggage-forwarding service (takkyubin) between Tokyo and Kyoto, so you travel with only a small bag.
