For a honeymoon that is warm, easy and not too far, Malaysia works well: three nights on Langkawi's beaches, then two in Kuala Lumpur. Budget roughly ₹60,000 to ₹1,00,000 per person for six days, flights included, and complete the free arrival card three days before you fly.

## Visa and entry rules for Indians

**Indian passport holders do not need a visa for Malaysia right now, but the waiver runs only until 31 December 2026 unless it is extended again.** The High Commission of Malaysia in New Delhi's [entry requirements page](https://www.kln.gov.my/web/ind_new-delhi/requirement_foreigner), which we checked on 8 October 2026, says the visa waiver for Indian nationals runs until 31 December 2026, for up to 30 days, for business, tourism, social or transit purposes. It asks Indian travellers to have supporting documents ready for screening:

1. **A passport valid for at least six months.**
2. **A confirmed return flight ticket.**
3. **A confirmed hotel booking.**
4. **The Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC)**, submitted online **three days before the arrival date**. It is free, one per traveller, at [imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main).

The same page lists "sufficient financial provisions" as a general entry condition but gives no cash amount. Carry a recent bank or card statement and some ringgit. Indian press has reported travellers being refused entry for missing documents, so keep the hotel voucher and tickets handy, even on a package.

> Use only the official portal for the MDAC, and never pay to "register" it. If you travel after 31 December 2026, check the [Malaysian Immigration Department](https://www.imi.gov.my/) first: you may need an eVisa.

## Day-by-day honeymoon itinerary

### Day 1: fly to Langkawi and settle in

Fly to Kuala Lumpur and connect to Langkawi, or look for a one-stop fare. Pick a resort on or near **Pantai Cenang** for restaurants and nightlife, or the quieter **Tanjung Rhu** side in the north for privacy. Watch sunset from the beach.

### Day 2: cable car and Sky Bridge

Go early to the **Langkawi Cable Car** at Oriental Village and the curved **Sky Bridge** on Gunung Mat Cincang, before cloud and queues build. Keep the afternoon for the pool. Check the operator's site for opening hours and ticket prices, because the bridge can close in bad weather.

### Day 3: island hopping or a sunset cruise

Book a private boat or a small-group island-hopping trip for the **Pulau Dayang Bunting** freshwater lake and a mangrove tour. Prefer a quiet evening? A sunset cruise suits couples better than a full-day group tour. Hire a car or use Grab for the rest; the island is small.

### Day 4: fly to Kuala Lumpur

Take a morning flight and check in near **KLCC** if you want the skyline from your window. In the evening, see the **Petronas Twin Towers** lit up and walk the park below them. Book the Observation Deck in advance for sunset.

### Day 5: city, food and shopping

Visit the **Batu Caves** early (climbing the temple stairs is free; dress modestly), then eat in **Brickfields** (Little India) and on **Jalan Alor**. Spend the afternoon in the Bukit Bintang malls, and end with a rooftop bar or a dinner if the budget allows.

### Day 6: fly home

Take the KLIA Ekspres train from KL Sentral to the airport, and aim to be there at least three hours before an international flight.

## Honeymoon tips

- **Ask the resort** about honeymoon extras such as a room upgrade, cake or private dinner when you book, and mention the occasion.
- **Dress:** beachwear is fine at resorts, but cover up at Batu Caves and mosques.
- **Alcohol** is expensive in Malaysia and not on sale everywhere, so check your resort's bar before you assume.
- **Money:** carry a forex or multi-currency card; don't depend on UPI at shops.
- **Plugs:** Malaysia uses the Type G (UK-style) plug, so pack an adapter.
- **SIM:** an eSIM bought before you land saves queueing at the airport.
- **Getting around:** Grab works across KL and Langkawi.

## Best time to go

**December to March** is generally the drier, sunnier period on Langkawi, and it overlaps Indian wedding season, so book flights and resorts early. In the wetter months showers are heavier but prices are lower. Kuala Lumpur sees afternoon storms most of the year.
