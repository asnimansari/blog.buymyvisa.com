You can see the Maldives for roughly ₹45,000 to ₹70,000 per person by staying in a guesthouse on a local island such as Maafushi, not in a private-island resort. The visa is free on arrival, but you must file an online Traveller Declaration first.

## Visa and entry rules for Indians

**Indian passport holders get a free tourist visa on arrival in the Maldives and must also file the free online Traveller Declaration.** The Maldives Department of Immigration's [tourist visa page](https://immigration.gov.mv/visa/tourist-visa), which we checked on 8 October 2026, lists what you need:

1. **A passport valid for at least one month.**
2. **A prepaid, confirmed booking at a registered facility.**
3. **Confirmed return tickets.**
4. **Sufficient funds for the stay.** Maldives mission pages we saw in search results give a guideline of US$100 plus US$50 a day, but the immigration page does not state an amount.
5. **A Traveller Declaration**, submitted electronically through the IMUGA portal up to 96 hours before arrival and shown at immigration. Each traveller, including children, files their own.

The page we fetched does not state the length of stay. Maldives mission pages give it as 30 days for most nationalities, so confirm it with the department if you plan a long trip. The department says the [Traveller Declaration](https://immigration.gov.mv/traveller-declaration) is free, needs no agent and is filed on the official portal at imuga.immigration.gov.mv. Immigration officers decide entry at the port, so keep the guesthouse confirmation and return ticket on your phone and on paper.

> Do not pay a website to file the declaration or arrange a "visa". The government form is free, and look-alike sites exist.

Flights are the biggest line, and fares are usually lower outside the December to April peak. Compare prices for your dates, and book three to four weeks or more ahead. A package may look cheaper, so check whether it includes the speedboat and the green tax.

## Day-by-day plan: 5 days on Maafushi

### Day 1: land and transfer

Fly into Velana International Airport on Hulhulé. After immigration, find your guesthouse's speedboat desk (a shared boat to Maafushi takes roughly 45 minutes). Ask the guesthouse in advance to meet you at the jetty. Spend the afternoon on the island's bikini beach, and eat dinner in the village.

### Day 2: snorkelling and a sandbank

Book a half-day trip through your guesthouse: reef snorkelling, a sandbank stop and often a turtle or stingray encounter. Compare quotes from two operators, and confirm that life jackets and a guide are included.

### Day 3: a quiet day, or a dive

Beginners can try a discovery dive at a local dive centre, but ask for certification details and safety rules first. Otherwise, rest on the beach, rent a kayak and swim at the bikini beach. Sunset is best from the island's far side.

### Day 4: a second island or a sunset cruise

Hop to a nearby local island by public ferry, or join a sunset dolphin cruise from Maafushi. Dolphin sightings are common on these cruises, though never guaranteed.

### Day 5: back to the airport

Take your pre-booked speedboat to the airport at least four hours before an international flight. Speedboats are for island hopping, not for last-minute connections, so leave a buffer.

## Rules on a local island

- **No alcohol** in guesthouse villages. It is sold at resorts, not on local islands. Check the current customs rules before you pack any.
- **Dress modestly** in the village, covering shoulders and knees. Swimwear belongs on the designated bikini beach.
- **Fridays** are quieter, and some ferries do not run.
- **Vegetarians** should tell the guesthouse in advance. Fish is the staple, and a kitchen used to Indian guests will cook rice, dal and vegetable curries.

## Best time to go

**December to April** is the dry, sunny season, with calmer seas and the highest prices. **May to November** is wetter and windier, with cheaper flights and rooms, so most budget travellers aim for the shoulder months, April and November. Check the forecast for snorkel trips, because rough seas can cancel them.

## Practical tips

- **Money:** US dollars are widely used. Carry a forex card, and keep some cash for small shops, because not every guesthouse takes cards or charges a card fee.
- **UPI:** do not rely on it. Ask your bank about international UPI before you go.
- **SIM:** a prepaid tourist SIM or an eSIM bought in advance avoids queues at the airport.
- **Plugs:** guesthouses use Type D, G and others, so carry a universal adapter.
- **Sun and reef:** use reef-safe sunscreen and do not touch coral.
