A seven-day Palawan trip means three nights in El Nido for lagoons and island-hopping, two in Coron for wreck snorkelling and lake swims, and a night in Manila at each end. Indians can enter the Philippines without a visa for up to 14 days, and a mid-range trip costs roughly ₹94,000 to ₹1,45,000 per person.

## Visa and entry rules for Indians

**Indian passport holders do not need a visa for a short tourist stay, but there are conditions.** The Philippine Embassy in Tokyo's [visa-free entry page](https://tokyo.philembassy.net/consular-section/services/visa/visa-free-entry-for-temporary-visits/), which we checked on 10 October 2026, says the following for Indian nationals under a policy effective 8 June 2025:

1. **14 days visa-free**, non-extendable and non-convertible to any other Philippine visa category.
2. **30 days instead of 14** if you hold a valid visa or permanent residence permit from the US, Japan, Australia, Canada, the Schengen area, Singapore or the UK.
3. **Documents to show:** a passport valid for at least six months beyond your stay, a confirmed hotel booking, proof of financial capacity and a return or onward ticket.
4. **No derogatory record** with the Bureau of Immigration.
5. **Transit:** the page says Indians passing through the Philippines to a third country need a Philippine transit visa before entering.

The Bureau of Immigration's [home page](https://immigration.gov.ph/) carried an advisory dated 5 October 2026 reminding arriving foreign passengers to register or update their information on [eTravel](https://etravel.gov.ph/) at least 72 hours before travel. The eTravel site says it is free. We could not open the Philippine Embassy in New Delhi's website, and the older notice we found there described a different version of the rule, so check with your airline or the Embassy before booking.

Beware of agents selling a "Philippines visa for Indians" or charging to file eTravel for you. Neither is required for this trip.

The cheap end takes shared-boat tours and fan or basic air-conditioned rooms; the high end adds private boats, beachfront resorts and flights between the two islands. Tour prices, ferry timetables and baggage allowances on small domestic airlines change, so confirm each before paying.

## Getting there from India

Air India flies non-stop from Delhi to Manila in roughly 7 hours. Philippine Airlines has announced Delhi and Mumbai flights from December 2026, subject to approvals, so more choice may arrive. From Mumbai, Bengaluru or Chennai, expect a one-stop route.

Palawan is a separate flight from Manila. Book it within the same trip: you can fly to El Nido directly, or to Puerto Princesa and take a van of five to six hours, and to Coron via Busuanga airport. Because domestic airlines enforce tight baggage limits, pack a soft bag and check the allowance before you buy.

## Day-by-day itinerary

### Day 1: arrive in Manila

Land, clear immigration with your hotel booking, return ticket and funds proof ready, and sleep near the airport. Do not squeeze a domestic connection into the same evening; a delayed international flight would cost you the onward seat.

### Day 2: fly to El Nido

Take the morning flight to El Nido and check in near the town or Corong-Corong beach. Walk to Las Cabanas or Nacpan if energy remains, and book your tours for the next two days.

### Day 3: island-hopping, lagoons

Tours in El Nido are sold as lettered routes. A typical day covers the Big Lagoon, Small Lagoon and Secret Lagoon, with a grilled-fish lunch on a beach and snorkelling stops. Kayaks are usually included at the lagoons. Go on an early boat to beat the crowds.

### Day 4: a second route or a rest day

Take a different tour route for other beaches and snorkelling spots, or hire a scooter or tricycle for Nacpan Beach, a long stretch of sand on the west coast. If seas are rough, boats do not sail, so keep one spare day.

### Day 5: ferry to Coron

Take the fast ferry from El Nido to Coron, a journey of several hours, and settle in Coron town. Check the operator's timetable for the season, since sailings are cancelled in bad weather. In the afternoon, climb the steps to Mount Tapyas for sunset and soak in the Maquinit hot springs.

### Day 6: Kayangan Lake and wrecks

Spend a day on the water around Coron: Kayangan Lake, Twin Lagoon and the Japanese wreck sites where snorkellers swim over sunken ships. Basic snorkelling is enough for the shallow wrecks, and boats provide life vests.

### Day 7: fly home via Manila

Fly from Busuanga to Manila and connect to Delhi. Leave a buffer of at least four hours between a domestic arrival and an international departure, or sleep a night in Manila to be safe. Airline baggage and weather delays are the usual causes of missed connections.

## Eating in the Philippines as an Indian traveller

Rice is the base of most meals, and menus lean on pork, chicken and fish. Vegetarians can ask for sautéed vegetables, egg dishes, fried rice without meat, and fruit shakes; confirm that sauces do not contain shrimp paste or fish sauce. Jain travellers will find it harder, so carry dry snacks for boat days. Manila has North Indian restaurants, and El Nido has a few, so a lunch of dal and roti is possible.

## Best time to go

**December to April** is the dry season with calm seas and the best conditions for island hopping; it is also the busiest and priciest period. **May** is hot and still mostly dry. **June to October** is the rainy and typhoon season: tours are cancelled on rough days and flights are delayed, so avoid it for a short, fixed-date trip.

## Practical tips

- **Money:** carry some pesos and withdraw more at ATMs in Manila, as El Nido and Coron have fewer machines and many tour operators want cash. Use a forex card for hotel bills and enable international use on your bank app. Do not count on UPI working at tour desks or small shops, so keep cash and a card as backup.
- **Connectivity:** buy an eSIM before you fly; coverage on boats and remote beaches is patchy.
- **Plugs:** sockets are mostly flat-pin, so Indian round-pin plugs will not fit; carry a universal adapter.
- **Insurance:** buy cover that includes weather cancellations and medical care on islands.
- **Reef-safe habits:** tour operators collect environmental fees; do not touch coral and use reef-safe sunscreen.
- **Safety:** use licensed boat operators with life jackets and avoid sailing when the coast guard suspends trips.
