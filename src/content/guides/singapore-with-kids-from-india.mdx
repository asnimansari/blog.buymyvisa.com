For a first trip abroad with children, Singapore is hard to beat: about five hours from most Indian metros, clean and safe, with a zoo, a night safari, a theme park and a garden of giant artificial trees on one small island. Five days is enough, and a family of four should budget roughly ₹65,000 to ₹95,000 per person including flights. The one thing to start early is the visa.

## Visa and entry rules for Indians

**Indian passport holders need a Singapore visa, and you can't get one at the airport.** ICA's [visa requirements page](https://www.ica.gov.sg/enter-transit-depart/entering-singapore/visa_requirements) lists India among the countries that need a visa and says applications go through an authorised visa agent appointed by the Singapore mission, or at the mission itself. That page does not state the fee, processing time or validity, so ask the agent for these in writing before you pay. Your length of stay is set by the visit pass issued at the checkpoint, not by the visa.

1. **A passport valid long enough for the trip** for every family member (agents usually ask for six months).
2. **An approved visa for each traveller**, children included.
3. **An SG Arrival Card for each traveller**, free, submitted online or in the MyICA app **within three days before arrival**, counting the arrival day. ICA does not endorse paid services for it, so skip any site that charges.
4. **A return ticket and proof of where you will stay**, which officers may ask to see.

> Start from the official site, [ica.gov.sg](https://www.ica.gov.sg/). Look-alike visa and arrival-card sites exist: use only an agent appointed by the Singapore mission in India, and never pay to submit the arrival card.

Transit is different. Indians may use the 96-hour Visa-Free Transit Facility only when travelling on to a third country, with an onward ticket and a valid visa or long-term pass (valid at least one more month) for Australia, Canada, Germany, Japan, New Zealand, Switzerland, the UK or the US. Don't plan a holiday on it.

## What it costs in rupees

Per person, a family of two adults and two children (about 6 to 12 years old), mid-range, five days. Singapore dollar amounts use roughly ₹65–70 per SGD; check the day's rate.

Ticket prices change, so confirm them on the official sites before booking. For orientation, 2026 visitor guides list Universal Studios Singapore at about S$83–86 for adults and S$62–69 for children aged 4–12, and Mandai's Night Safari page lists non-resident tickets at S$58 for adults and S$41 for children aged 3–12. Mandai also sells combined tickets for the Zoo, River Safari, Night Safari and Bird Paradise, which are worth comparing with single tickets.

## Day-by-day family itinerary

### Day 1: arrive and Gardens by the Bay

Changi is an attraction on its own, with a butterfly garden and the Jewel waterfall for tired children. After check-in, go to **Gardens by the Bay**. The outdoor gardens are free, and the Supertree Grove is at its best at dusk, when the lights come on. Dinner at a hawker centre such as **Lau Pa Sat** keeps the bill low.

### Day 2: Singapore Zoo and Night Safari

Go to the **Mandai** wildlife area in the morning, when animals are active and it is cooler. The **Singapore Zoo** is open-concept, and its water-play area cools children down. Return to the hotel for a nap, then come back for the **Night Safari** and its tram ride, and plan to leave by about 9:30 pm so children are not asleep on their feet. Buy tickets online ahead.

### Day 3: Sentosa

Take the Sentosa Express or the cable car over. Choose one main attraction: **Universal Studios Singapore** for children old enough for rides, or the **SEA Aquarium** and the beaches (Siloso, Palawan) for younger ones. Pre-book tickets to skip the queue.

### Day 4: Chinatown, Little India and a slow afternoon

Walk through **Chinatown** and the **Buddha Tooth Relic Temple**, then go to **Little India** for a vegetarian lunch and shopping at the Mustafa Centre, which stays open very late. Keep the afternoon free for the hotel pool or the Merlion and the Marina Bay waterfront.

### Day 5: last morning and flight home

Do a final walk at **Jewel** or **Merlion Park**, and leave for Changi at least three hours before your flight. The MRT from the city takes under an hour.

## Tips for families from India

- **Food:** vegetarian families should head to Little India and ask hawker stalls about fish sauce and egg. Halal and vegetarian options are common.
- **Money:** use a forex or multi-currency card. UPI works at some Singapore merchants, but don't depend on it. Carry a few notes for hawker stalls.
- **Plugs:** Singapore uses the Type G (UK-style) plug, so pack an adapter.
- **Transport:** the MRT and buses accept contactless bank cards; taxis cost more, and ride-hailing apps are common. Check the operator's rules for children's fares.
- **SIM:** an eSIM or an airport SIM is simple and cheap.
- **Rules:** chewing gum sales are banned, littering is fined heavily, and vapes are prohibited, so don't pack them.

## Best time to visit

**February to July** is usually drier, though short thunderstorms can hit at any time of year, since Singapore sits near the equator. The year-end monsoon brings more rain, and Indian school holidays (April to June) and Diwali week fill hotels and raise flight prices, so book early. It is hot and humid all year, so plan outdoor visits for mornings and evenings.
