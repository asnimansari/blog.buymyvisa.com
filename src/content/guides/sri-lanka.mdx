Sri Lanka is the easiest "foreign" trip from south India: an hour's flight, a free ETA, food that feels familiar with a twist, and the same time zone. In a week you can climb a 5th-century rock fortress, ride one of the world's most beautiful train lines through tea country, and end on the ramparts of a Dutch fort by the sea.

## Visa and entry rules for Indians

**Indian tourists need an ETA, and it is currently free.** Apply online at [eta.gov.lk](https://www.eta.gov.lk/) before you fly; most approvals arrive within a day or two. The tourist ETA allows **30 days** with a **double entry**.

1. **A passport valid for at least six months** from arrival.
2. **An approved ETA.** Your airline checks it at check-in in India, so don't count on getting one on arrival.
3. **A return ticket and enough money for your stay**, which immigration may ask to see.

> Sri Lanka has changed its ETA fees and system several times. Apply only on **eta.gov.lk**: look-alike sites charge a "service fee" for the same free form.

Indian rupees are not widely accepted in Sri Lanka despite what you may hear. Carry a forex card or US dollars to change, and use ATMs in town.

## Day-by-day itinerary

### Day 1: Colombo

Land at Bandaranaike airport, meet your driver and spend the afternoon in **Colombo**: the Galle Face Green promenade at sunset, the Pettah market, and dinner at a seafood place in Colombo 3. If your flight is late, skip Colombo and drive straight towards Sigiriya.

### Days 2–3: Sigiriya and Dambulla

Drive four hours north into the **Cultural Triangle**. Climb **Sigiriya** at opening time (7 am) before the heat and the crowds: past the frescoes and the giant lion's paws, about 1,200 steps to the palace ruins on the summit. Foreigners pay a dollar-priced ticket; SAARC passport holders get a lower rate, so carry your passport.

Next morning, visit the **Dambulla cave temples** (painted caves full of Buddha statues), or climb **Pidurangala** for the best view _of_ Sigiriya at sunrise.

### Day 4: Kandy

Drive to **Kandy**, the hill capital. Visit the **Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic** for the evening _puja_, walk around Kandy Lake, and see the botanical gardens at Peradeniya if you're keen on plants.

### Day 5: the train to Ella

The **Kandy to Ella train** takes about seven hours through tea estates, waterfalls and cloud forest. It's slow on purpose. Sit on the right-hand side for the best views, and buy a packet of _isso vadai_ when the vendors walk through.

### Days 6–7: the south coast and Galle

Your driver takes you down to the coast (about five hours), past the Ravana falls and, if you like, a stop at **Udawalawe** for a safari to see elephants. Spend the last two days in and around **Galle**: walk the ramparts of **Galle Fort** at sunset, swim at **Unawatuna** or **Mirissa**, and watch the stilt fishermen at Koggala.

## Best time to visit

**December to April** is dry on the west and south coasts and in the hill country, which is exactly this route. **May to September** brings the south-west monsoon here, but it's the best season on the east coast, so swap Galle for **Trincomalee** or **Arugam Bay** then. The hill country is cool all year: pack a light jacket for Ella.

## Getting there from India

SriLankan Airlines, Air India and IndiGo fly to Colombo from Chennai, Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi, Kochi, Thiruvananthapuram and Hyderabad. From the south, the flight is shorter than the drive to the airport. There's also a passenger ferry from **Nagapattinam** to **Kankesanthurai** in the north, but sailings depend on the season.
