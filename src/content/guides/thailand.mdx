Thailand is most Indians' first trip abroad for good reason: it's a four-hour flight, the food is easy to love, and you can be on a white-sand beach under a limestone cliff by day three. This plan splits a short week between **Bangkok** (temples, markets, the river) and the **Krabi** coast (Railay's cliffs and a day on the **Phi Phi Islands**), with one domestic flight between them.

## Visa and entry rules for Indians

**You don't need a visa for a holiday of up to 30 days.** Thailand cut visa-free stays for Indian passports from 60 to 30 days for arrivals on or after **15 September 2026**. For a 6-day trip, nothing changes in practice. Here's what you do need:

1. **A passport valid for at least six months** from the day you land, with a blank page.
2. **A Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC)** for every traveller. It is free, filed online at [tdac.immigration.go.th](https://tdac.immigration.go.th/), and opens **three days before you arrive**. You'll give your flight, where you're staying, and your trip details; save the confirmation it emails you.
3. **Proof that you'll leave and can pay your way.** Officers can ask for your return ticket, hotel bookings and funds. Keep them on your phone, plus a printout of the return ticket.

> The rules changed several times in 2026. Check the [Royal Thai Embassy, New Delhi](https://newdelhi.thaiembassy.org/) before you book, and again a week before you fly.

Watch out for look-alike "TDAC" sites that charge a fee: the official form is free and ends in **.go.th**.

## Day-by-day itinerary

### Days 1–2: Bangkok

Land at Suvarnabhumi in the morning (most overnight flights from India arrive around 6 am), drop your bags and take the Airport Rail Link into town. Spend the afternoon at the **Grand Palace** and **Wat Pho**, then cross the river by the 5-baht ferry to **Wat Arun** for sunset.

Day 2 is for markets and food. Do **Chatuchak** if it's a weekend, take a long-tail boat through the **Thonburi canals**, and finish in **Yaowarat** (Chinatown) for street food. Vegetarians: ask for _jay_ food (look for yellow flags) and say _mai sai nam pla_ (no fish sauce).

### Days 3–4: Krabi and Railay

Take an early flight to Krabi (1 h 20 m), then a taxi to **Ao Nang** and a long-tail boat to **Railay**. Railay has no roads, only cliffs, beaches and rock climbers. Walk to **Phra Nang beach** for the famous cave and karsts, and climb the short, muddy trail to the viewpoint if you have proper shoes.

On day 4, kayak the mangroves at **Ao Thalane** or take the **four-islands** tour (Poda, Chicken Island, Tup and Phra Nang) by long-tail boat.

### Day 5: Phi Phi Islands

A full-day speedboat tour from Ao Nang covers **Phi Phi Don**, **Maya Bay** (no swimming; the bay is protected and you view it from a boardwalk), **Pileh Lagoon** and a snorkelling stop. Book a tour that leaves before 8 am to beat the crowds.

<p class="note">
  Tip: carry a dry bag and reef-safe sunscreen. The marine-park entry fee is often collected in cash on the
  boat.
</p>

### Day 6: back to Bangkok and home

Fly Krabi to Bangkok, then on to India the same evening. If your flight home is late, spend the gap at **Iconsiam** on the river or get a Thai massage near the airport.

## Best time to visit

**November to March** is the sweet spot: dry, about 30 °C, and calm on the Andaman Sea. **April** is very hot. **May to October** is monsoon on the Krabi coast. Hotels are cheaper, but rough seas cancel island tours, so swap Krabi for the Gulf side (Koh Samui) if you must go then.

## Getting there from India

Direct flights to Bangkok leave from Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Kolkata, Hyderabad and more, run by IndiGo, Air India, Thai Airways and Thai AirAsia. Several metros also have direct flights to **Phuket**, about three hours by road from Krabi. Flying into Phuket and out of Bangkok saves you a domestic flight.
