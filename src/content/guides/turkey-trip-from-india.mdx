For a first trip to Turkey, spend 3 nights in Istanbul and 3 in Cappadocia, and budget roughly ₹85,000 to ₹135,000 per person. The step to sort out first is the visa: Indians cannot just turn up, and the easy online e-Visa is open only to those who already hold a Schengen, US, UK or Ireland visa or residence permit.

## Visa and entry rules for Indians

**A visa is required before you fly.** We checked these official pages on 9 October 2026:

- The Turkish foreign ministry's [Visa Information for Foreigners](https://www.mfa.gov.tr/visa-information-for-foreigners.en.mfa) says ordinary passport holders from India need a visa. The e-Visa is open to those with a valid Schengen, US, UK or Ireland visa or residence permit, is single entry and is valid for one month, for tourism or commerce only.
- The Turkish Embassy in New Delhi's [announcement of 1 March 2024](https://newdelhi-emb.mfa.gov.tr/Mission/ShowAnnouncement/361514) adds the detail: a used or unused supporting visa is accepted if its validity covers your entry date; e-visas from other countries are not accepted; if the supporting visa is in an old passport, register the e-Visa on the new passport and carry both. The e-Visa site does not verify the documents, so the airline and Turkish immigration do, and the airline may refuse boarding.
- **Everyone else** applies for a sticker visa through the Turkish mission, starting with the pre-application system the ministry links from its [visa pages](https://www.mfa.gov.tr/pre-application-system-for-turkish-sticker-visa.en.mfa). Leave several weeks.

**Fees:** neither official page we could read states the amount for Indian passports, and the ministry's fee PDF was unreadable to us. The embassy notice only says the e-Visa costs at least US$85 less than a sticker visa. See the price on the official site before you pay and do not trust a figure from a blog, including ours. We also could not confirm processing times from an official page.

**Apply only at [evisa.gov.tr](https://www.evisa.gov.tr/en/).** The embassy warns that other sites offering e-visas are fake or charge more than twice the official fee. Your passport should be valid for at least 60 days beyond your stay, and the ministry advises six months.

## Day-by-day itinerary

### Day 1: arrive in Istanbul

Fly in, take the metro or an airport shuttle to Sultanahmet, and rest. In the evening, walk to the waterfront at Eminönü and eat a fish sandwich by the Galata Bridge.

### Day 2: the old city

Visit **Hagia Sophia**, the **Blue Mosque** (dress modestly, remove shoes), the **Basilica Cistern** and then **Topkapı Palace**. Mosque visits pause around prayer times, so check hours. End at the **Grand Bazaar** or the quieter Spice Bazaar. Haggle politely and start at about half the first price.

### Day 3: Bosphorus and Beyoğlu

Take a public ferry up the Bosphorus, much cheaper than the tourist cruises. Cross to Karaköy, climb to the **Galata Tower**, and walk İstiklal Avenue. Take the tram back in the evening.

### Day 4: fly to Cappadocia

Take a morning flight to Kayseri or Nevşehir and a shuttle to **Göreme** (about an hour). Walk the **Göreme Open-Air Museum** with its rock-cut churches, then watch sunset from Love Valley or the Red Valley trails.

### Day 5: sunrise balloons and valleys

The balloon flight leaves before dawn and lasts about an hour. Flights are cancelled in high wind, so keep Day 6 free as a spare. Spend the afternoon on a guided or self-driven loop: **Uçhisar Castle**, **Pasabag** and **Devrent** valley with their fairy chimneys, and the underground city at **Derinkuyu**.

### Day 6: spare balloon morning, then Ihlara or a pottery stop

If Day 5 was cancelled, fly now. Otherwise walk the **Ihlara Valley** or visit the pottery workshops in Avanos. Fly back to Istanbul in the evening.

### Day 7: fly home

Allow three hours at the airport for an international flight. Note that Istanbul has two airports, so confirm which one your flight uses.

## Eating as an Indian traveller

Vegetarians do fine: red lentil soup (mercimek çorbası), menemen, mezes, stuffed vine leaves and börek are meat-free, and pide with cheese is on every menu. Ask whether stock or lard is used. Jain food is hard to find, so carry dry snacks. Halal meat is standard, and Indian restaurants in Istanbul's tourist districts are an easy fallback.

## Best time to go

**April, May, September and October** have mild weather. **June to August** is hot in Istanbul and crowded. **November to March** is cold, with snow in Cappadocia, and more balloon flights are cancelled.

## Practical tips

- **Money:** carry a forex card for most spending and some lira in cash for bazaars and taxis. UPI is not accepted.
- **SIM:** an eSIM bought before you fly is easiest.
- **Plugs:** Turkey uses the European two-pin plug (Type C or F), so pack an adapter.
- **Getting around:** Istanbul has metro, tram and ferries on a rechargeable card. In Cappadocia, buy a tour or rent a car.
- **Documents:** keep your visa, passport, hotel bookings and return ticket together. If you rely on a Schengen, US or UK visa, carry the original.
